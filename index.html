About

I’m finishing an undergraduate degree in artificial intelligence and a master’s in robotics at Penn (May 2027). I work in the GRASP Lab with Alejandro Ribeiro and Pratik Chaudhari.

I study what a learned model of a physical system should represent, such as objects, contacts, and fast motion, so that a robot can plan with it. I also want that model to keep working when the task, the robot, or the number of objects changes.

I came to this question through graph neural networks. I studied why graph transformers trained on small graphs keep working on larger ones, and my master’s thesis builds a graph foundation model that carries across tasks and domains. I then moved to the physical world. I built a predictive model for event cameras, which record fast motion as a stream of brightness changes instead of frames, and I’m now adding memory to that representation for localization on fast drones. For my senior design project, I’m building the perception side of 3D reconstruction for a robotic hand.

I’m applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.
